# Life Commander — Market Research (2026)

## Goal
Build a mass-market personal productivity application that can be sold as a one-time digital product and run locally/offline without our VPS, mandatory account, subscription, or paid API.

## Market signals

### Notion
Notion currently offers a free individual plan and paid Plus/Business tiers. Its product covers pages, databases, subtasks, dependencies, properties, calendars and offline capabilities. This validates broad demand for a flexible personal workspace, but also means a direct Notion clone would compete with a mature free product.

Source: Notion Pricing, checked September 2026.
https://www.notion.com/pricing

### Digital planner market
Etsy currently shows 5,000+ digital productivity planner items. Current listings span very low-cost PDFs and higher-priced all-in-one planners. Examples visible in the current marketplace include approximately $6, $9, $14, $18, $22 and $26 products, with some heavily reviewed listings having hundreds or thousands of reviews.

Source: Etsy Digital Productivity Planner marketplace, checked September 2026.
https://www.etsy.com/market/digital_productivity_planner

### One-time life-planner products
Gumroad already has one-time products positioned as life planners. A current Life Planner listing is $24 with lifetime access and 18 ratings. Another current HTML-based life-management planner explicitly sells as a single offline HTML file, with no subscription, no login, local storage and one-time purchase.

## Competitive categories

1. Flexible workspaces: Notion
2. Task managers: Todoist, TickTick
3. Time/routine planners: Structured, Routinery
4. Digital PDF planners: Etsy / GoodNotes ecosystem
5. Notion life-system templates: Gumroad/Etsy
6. Offline HTML planners: small but directly relevant niche

## Opportunity

The strongest differentiation is not "another task manager". The product should combine the visual clarity of a daily planner with the breadth of a personal life system:

Today -> Tasks -> Calendar -> Habits -> Goals -> Projects -> Notes -> Journal -> Finances -> Routines -> Analytics.

The important commercial distinction is that the buyer receives a finished application rather than a blank framework that must be configured.

## Product principles

- Local-first.
- No mandatory account.
- No subscription.
- No mandatory cloud.
- Export/import and backup from day one.
- Responsive desktop/tablet/mobile UI.
- Five languages built into the architecture: English, Russian, Spanish, German, French.
- All visible text must come from localization keys.
- No language-specific strings embedded in components.
- The product must work without an internet connection after download.
- The commercial package should be a downloadable product, not a hosted SaaS.

## Risks

- The productivity category is crowded.
- Free products create strong price pressure.
- PDF planners are much cheaper, so the HTML application must feel substantially more useful.
- A one-time product has limited recurring revenue, so distribution and positioning matter.
- We should not promise income or sales volume; pricing is a market-positioning decision, not a sales guarantee.

## Recommended differentiation

1. Premium "Personal Life OS" positioning.
2. Very fast Today dashboard.
3. Visual time blocking.
4. Goals connected to projects and habits.
5. Weekly review and personal analytics.
6. Personal finance without becoming accounting software.
7. Local privacy: data stays on the buyer's device.
8. Backup/export/import.
9. Five-language UI.
10. Beautiful enough to be used every day, simple enough to understand in minutes.

## Research conclusion

The market validates demand for planners and one-time productivity systems. The gap worth testing is a polished, application-like, offline personal Life OS that is easier to use than a blank Notion workspace and more powerful than a static PDF planner.
